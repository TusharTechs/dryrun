3 hedges · mostly "just"

Your hedging is underlined as you type

Just. Maybe. Sorry to bother you. I might be wrong but. Marked the moment you write them, with a running count of the one you lean on most. Not blocked — marked. The point is to see the shape of your own flinching before you send it, not to be corrected afterwards.

The detector runs entirely on your phone, so it never waits on a network and never sends anything to find a hedge.

And when you finally say it straight, they say nothing

Three full seconds of dead air. Most people fill it — and undo the thing they just did well. Sitting in the silence is the actual skill, so the app measures whether you did, on the device, as a fact rather than a model's opinion.

Then it tells you what you actually did

Four things, each quoted back to you word for word from your own transcript. No score out of a hundred, no grade, no encouragement you didn't earn.

Run it again and DryRun shows you the line that changed — your hedged version and your straight one, side by side. That comparison is the whole app.

What you type stays on your phone

The conversation you're rehearsing is about a real colleague. So there's no account, no name, no email — and no share button anywhere in the app, on purpose. Transcripts and scores never leave the device, and messages are sent only to generate a reply and are never stored. The privacy policy says exactly what that means, in the same plain words.

Built in the open

Kotlin Multiplatform — nearly all of the code is written once and runs on both Android and iOS, including every screen. The API is a Cloudflare Worker and subscriptions run through RevenueCat.
